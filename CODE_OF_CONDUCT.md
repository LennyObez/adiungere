# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be someone people want to work with. Assume the other person is competent and acting in good faith. Argue
about the code, never about the person writing it.

## What is expected

- **Critique the work, not its author.** "This copies the sample table before the offsets are promoted" is a
  review. "You clearly did not think about this" is not.
- **Say what you actually verified.** A claim without the command that produced it wastes everyone's time and
  occasionally sends someone down a false trail for a day. This project is built on that distinction.
- **Accept correction.** Being wrong in public is the normal cost of working in the open. Nobody here loses
  standing by saying so.
- **Give people room.** Contributors work in different time zones, in second or third languages, and around
  the rest of their lives. Silence is usually a schedule, not an insult.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment of any kind. Personal attacks. Demeaning comments about who someone is rather than what they
wrote. Sustained disruption of a discussion. Publishing someone's private information. Unwelcome sexual
attention.

One thing is specific to this project. **Never post a recording, or a frame of one, in an issue, a pull
request or a discussion.** Dashcam footage carries a route, satellite positions and other people's vehicles
and faces, and the people in it never agreed to appear here. Everything a report needs can be given as
structure rather than content, and the issue form says how.

## Scope

This applies in the repository, in its issues, pull requests and discussions, and anywhere someone
represents the project.

## Reporting

Report a problem privately to the maintainer at conduct@adiungere.com. That address reaches one person and
nobody else, and it is separate from the security contact so that a report about behaviour does not land in
a tool built for vulnerabilities. Reports are handled confidentially. Consequences range from a request to
change behaviour, through temporary exclusion from a discussion, to a permanent ban, proportionate to what
happened and whether it continued after being raised.

The maintainer is subject to this document on the same terms as everyone else.
